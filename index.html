Dual-spring slide balancer

The part that started the catalog. A slide that travels vertically on the press has to fight its own weight. The SB cancels that weight, so the slide, retainer and angle pin can be designed as if gravity were not there.

SB · sizes S–XL · stroke 20–120 mm · 100 % mechanical
Withoutslide drops, Δ ≠ 0

Once the angle pin leaves the slide, nothing holds it but the retainer. It drifts down, the retainer wears, and the pin takes a side load on every close.

With SBheld, Δ = 0

Two opposed die springs push on a floating rod bolted to the slide back. Preload equals the slide weight at mid-stroke and stays within ±5 % over the travel.

How a part gets into the catalog

From a floor fix to a part number.

We do not start from a product idea. We start from a mold that is giving someone trouble. The balancer began as a counterweight welded onto a 22 kg slide in a customer's tool. It worked, it was ugly, and the next mold needed a different one.

So we drew the mechanism as a cartridge, set the spring preload from the slide mass instead of by trial, ran it on the live mold for a full maintenance interval, then sized it S to XL. Now it is a part number with a pocket drawing, and the next slide takes an afternoon instead of a week.

Every component on this site went through the same four steps. If you have a mold with a recurring problem and no standard part for it, that is exactly what we want to hear about.

DFM review

What we check.

The review is written for the person who owns the part, not for a toolmaker. Every finding says what it costs in the mold (a slide, a lifter, a longer cycle) and what change to the part would remove it.

Where a finding points at a recurring problem, the answer is usually a catalog part. A vertical slide gets an SB balancer, a grease-sensitive part gets GB bushings, a rib that burns gets a VI vent. The report links to the part number.
